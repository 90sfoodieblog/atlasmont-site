Black Las Vegas: Concentration and Share

Where the general meets the particular is the table of Las Vegas; read the two together. In the Las Vegas metro, Black residents number about 330,000 and form 14.5% of the population on the alone-or-in-combination count — against 10% for the state as a whole on the alone count, and 13.5% for the United States.

The Share and What It Builds

A share is not a statistic in the abstract; at 14.5%, it is the composition of every school, congregation, electorate-sized gathering, and customer base in the metro. In Las Vegas, shares at this level sustain institutions — media, congregations, businesses, and political organizations — that smaller shares cannot, and they shape how a metro appears in every national table this atlas publishes. The Las Vegas figure also explains the metro's gravitational pull in the movement plates: concentration is itself an attraction, drawing new residents toward an established community with its institutions already built.

Employment in Black Las Vegas runs through the hospitality economy that employs the Valley generally — the resorts' culinary, housekeeping, security, and entertainment workforces, substantially unionized on the Culinary Workers' model, which the labor data record as among the strongest private-sector wage floors for service work in the nation — and through the public sector, health care, logistics (the Valley is a distribution node for the whole Southwest), and Nellis Air Force Base's military economy.
