Black Tampa: Concentration and Share

In Tampa, concentration is the metro question in its purest form: how much of a place is Black, and how that share compares with the state and the nation around it. In the Tampa metro, Black residents number about 95,000 and form 26% of the population on the alone-or-in-combination count — against 16% for the state as a whole on the alone count, and 13.5% for the United States.

The Share and What It Builds

A share is not a statistic in the abstract; at 26%, it is the composition of every school, congregation, electorate-sized gathering, and customer base in the metro. In Tampa, shares at this level sustain institutions — media, congregations, businesses, and political organizations — that smaller shares cannot, and they shape how a metro appears in every national table this atlas publishes. The Tampa figure also explains the metro's gravitational pull in the movement plates: concentration is itself an attraction, drawing new residents toward an established community with its institutions already built.
